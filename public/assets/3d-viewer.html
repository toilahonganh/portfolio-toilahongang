History of the Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon

The Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon, often simply referred to as Saigon Notre-Dame, is a French-colonial Roman Catholic cathedral located in Ho Chi Minh City (formerly Saigon), Vietnam. It was built between 1877 and 1883 by French colonists. The cathedral is known for its French-style architecture, which is a blend of Gothic and Romanesque styles. It has become one of the most iconic landmarks in the city and is considered a symbol of both the colonial period and the city’s cultural heritage.

The cathedral's distinctive features include two 40-meter-high bell towers, which are among the tallest in Vietnam, and a beautiful stained glass window that depicts the Virgin Mary. The cathedral remains an important religious center in the city, serving both as a place of worship and a popular tourist attraction.
